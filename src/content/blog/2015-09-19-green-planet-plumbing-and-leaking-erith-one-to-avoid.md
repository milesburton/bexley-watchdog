<p>Who&#8217;d have thought there&#8217;s be a third week of nightmares on Bexley Watchdog. Green Planet Plumbing and Heating is under the spotlight tonight (abbreviated)</p>
<p>Looking for tradesmen, I&#8217;d much rather get a local engineer in than leave it to one of the high street names.</p>
<p>I originally brought in <a href="http://www.greenplanetplumbing.co.uk/">Green Planet and Heating</a>. Based just around the corner from me, I asked them to quote for a major GCH upgrade worth around four grand. Initially I was quite impressed, Brian Meacock, the director spotted several far less intrusive route for my upgrade than his competitors. However, that introduction soon went south.</p>
<p>&#8230;Let&#8217;s fast forward&#8230;</p>
<p>&#8230;9 months rolls around since moving in. I receive my first water bill &#8211; it is literally multiples over my previous property &#8211; great. After moving into my new house, they&#8217;d failed to mention a rather substantial leak.</p>
<p>&lt;insert Thames Water and various other investigations [we&#8217;ll pick this up later]&gt;</p>
<p>Worried, as you can imagine I called Brian Meacock back. After a short time he confirmed the problem must be between the water main and the kitchen stop-cock, and quoted accordingly.</p>
<p>Fantastic I thought. Green Planet Plumbing and Heating know what they are doing so I agreed for them to carry out the job. Works started well, a new main was re-routed to kitchen. Took about a day, thank god that&#8217;s sorted&#8230;</p>
<p>Two weeks later, I took a measurement.</p>
<p>I&#8217;m wasting more water than before this all started.  I called Brian Meacock and he arrives quickly, he confirms despite his fix the leak must be between the stop cock and the main water meter. I call out Thames Water who arrived <em>many</em> weeks later, and tell me &#8211; no it must be inside.</p>
<p>I call Green Planet, but Brian Meacock was on holiday so I call <a href="http://www.unitec-uk.com/">Unitec</a>. After a short quote, Scott [director] of Unitec shows up and diagnoses the problem &#8211; the lead pipe I asked Green Planet to replace during their fix has failed.  <em>(if you&#8217;re not aware, lead was used for pipe work decades back, it&#8217;s has lasted through the years because it isn&#8217;t disturbed &#8211; unfortunately when it is touched or manhandled that seal is broken).</em></p>
<p>Turns out Green Planet Plumbing and Heating didn&#8217;t bother to repair roughly a foot of lead. After their works that tiny bit of lead was disturbed and breached almost immediately. That breach threw a heavy spray of water under my hall and living room damaging wall paper, floor boards and electrics.</p>
<p>Turns out Green Planet made three major errors.</p>
<ol>
<li><em>&#8220;Confirmed&#8221;</em> the leak was between the outdoor stockcock and the water meter</li>
<li>They had routed passed the outdoor stop cock and had not communicated that to Brian Meacock</li>
<li>Failing to accept any wrong-doing (See email)</li>
</ol>
<p>I dropped Brian Meacock of Green Planet a simple email asking him to cover the cost of the remedial works (£100).  <a href="https://bexleywatchdog.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/email.docx">And this is how he responded</a></p>
<p>I suggest you avoid Green Planet Plumbing and Heating.</p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p>[Update]</p>
<p>After significant effort, Brian finally gave me the ~£100 I asked for leaving me with a salesmen apology, and significant damage to my hardwood, wall paper and skirting.</p>
<p>I&#8217;ve had several plumbers quote for more recent jobs, each and every one has quoted on how poor Green Planet&#8217;s work was.</p>
<p>With this that in mind, avoid &#8211; or if you&#8217;re brave, pay after the fact.</p>
